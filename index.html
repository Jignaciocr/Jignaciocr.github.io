PhD project · 2023–2027

Working memory, with and without consciousness

My PhD studies the computational principles of consciousness through working memory: can working memory operate on information we are not aware of? I build biologically constrained network models of unconscious working memory experiments and contrast them with human findings, looking for the mechanisms that could explain them. Two projects feed this question.

First authorPreprint

Reluctant to ReLU: uncontrolled connectivity pruning in trainable excitatory–inhibitory RNNs

Castillejo & Vadillo (2026). Preprint, PsyArXiv.

We studied a paradigm of trainable E–I networks and identified a potential source of bias in computational experiments with them. Dale’s principle is enforced by passing the weights through a ReLU: one technical choice among hundreds, easily overlooked, yet with significant consequences. A weight that reaches zero can never recover, so connectivity is silently pruned, training slows and dynamics change. The absolute value avoids it.

Methods in this study

  • Model: trainable E–I RNNs with Tsodyks–Markram synapses (Song et al., 2016; Masse et al., 2019).
  • Analyses: pruning statistics, training efficiency, decoding of neuronal and synaptic states.
  • Code: Python and TensorFlow 1.x simulations adapted from Masse et al. (2019); R for the analyses.

Looking ahead

Future directions

My PhD aims to understand cognition and consciousness from a cross-level perspective: cellular and synaptic properties, network dynamics, representations and behaviour. This may translate into three new research lines.

Next-gen AI inspired by Biology ML and Neuroscience parted ways, but brain computation still holds secrets that could revolutionize AI architectures.
Translating theory into treatment Cognitive Neuroscience is extremely hard for the same reason it is powerful: full explanations must link cognition from the protein up to the living organism. Such understanding may enable new treatments for neurological diseases.
Consciousness in brains and machines What neurocomputational principles give rise to consciousness? I want to uncover them in human brain data and test whether the same principles emerge in AI, crossing brain recordings with mechanistic interpretability. This could let us ask, precisely, whether an AI is conscious.
